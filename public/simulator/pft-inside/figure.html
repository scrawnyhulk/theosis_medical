Personal fat threshold: How type 2 diabetes develops

An interactive model of how overflowing fat storage can lead to type 2 diabetes, and how that path can run in reverse.

What does this mean?

Choose a threshold, then slide upward to add fuel: glycogen first, then fat under the skin, muscle, around the organs, the liver and the pancreas in this teaching sequence. Higher thresholds delay glucose changes in this fictional example. The scale is not time, calories, or pounds.

Reversal in real life

In the DiRECT trial, an ~850 kcal/day diet-replacement programme put 46% of people in remission at 12 months and 36% at 24 months (HbA1c below 6.5% off all diabetes medicines).

Best odds: diabetes for under 6 years, losing 15 kg or more (86% remission), and beta cells that recover. Remission is not a cure; keep checking A1c.

Lean et al., Lancet 2018; Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2019 · Taylor et al., Cell Metab 2018. The slider cannot predict remission.

What this stage means & how to read the views

Liver and muscle hold glucose as glycogen. This example starts with partly stocked reserves. Move the slider to watch the teal glycogen highlights build before added fat appears.

Replenishing glycogen is normal. It is not a step into diabetes.

Extreme threshold / hyperplasia. Adipocyte hyperplasia adds fat cells that can store energy. This helps explain why a person with substantial obesity may not have diabetes. This is a possible phenotype, not a guarantee of lasting protection or absence of other health risks. The cell counts are schematic.
